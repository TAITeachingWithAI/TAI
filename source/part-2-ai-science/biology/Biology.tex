Subject: Biology


\vspace{0.3cm}

Activity duration: 90 min

\vspace{0.5cm}

Steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Life on Earth, what does it require? (15 min)
    \item Investigating your exoplanet's extreme condition with a real scientific paper (50 min)
    \item Design an organism for your planet (25 min)
\end{enumerate}

Learning goals:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Learn about the main requirements for life.
\item Understand the main principles of how evolution shapes an organism's traits based on the environmental conditions.
\item Practice using AI to help read and understand a scientific paper, connecting it to biology concepts from the curriculum.
\item Reflect and critically analyze the output of the AI.
\item Apply the above to design an organism under certain conditions, and critically evaluate an AI-generated one.



\end{itemize}

\textcolor{accentcolor}{\textbf{\faLightbulb\ Initial ideas}}

\question{What are the basic conditions that would need to be present at the exoplanet for life to survive there? Explain why each condition is important.}

\begin{solution}
Based on every form of life scientists have ever studied on Earth (and everything we know of chemistry and physics), life needs: 
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{liquid solvent} in which molecules can move and react -- on Earth this is always water, though scientists debate whether other liquids (like liquid ammonia or methane) could in principle work elsewhere
  \item The right \textbf{chemical building blocks}, above all carbon, which can form the huge variety of stable, complex molecules (proteins, DNA, lipids) that life is built from, together with hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus and sulfur (CHNOPS). Each of these elements play an essential role in building and maintaining living organisms. \textbf{Carbon} is the backbone of biological molecules, with four bonds, it is able to form huge and complex molecules. \textbf{Hydrogen and oxygen} form water and many organic molecules, and oxygen is also used in respiration to release energy from food. \textbf{Nitrogen} is needed to make amino acids (which build proteins) and DNA/RNA (genetic material). \textbf{Phosphorus} is part of the DNA, cell membranes, and ATP for energy transfer, and \textbf{sulfur} is found in some amino acids. It forms strong links that help proteins keep their correct shape. Together, they provide the right balance of stability and reactivity, allowing cells to grow, reproduce, and carry out the chemical reactions needed for life. Although scientists are exploring whether life elsewhere could use different chemistry, all known life on Earth depends on these six elements.

  \item A \textbf{usable source of energy}, either light (photosynthesis) or chemical energy from reactions between substances (chemosynthesis), to power metabolism.

  \item A \textbf{stable-enough temperature range} -- too hot and the molecules that build cells (proteins, DNA) fall apart faster than they can be repaired; too cold and the chemical reactions of metabolism become too slow to sustain life.

  \item Enough \textbf{protection from destructive radiation}, since radiation damages DNA faster than most organisms can repair it. 
\end{itemize}
Every organism we know of, however extreme its environment, still meets all five of these conditions -- it simply has evolved unusual ways of meeting them.
\end{solution}
