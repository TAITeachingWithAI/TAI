# Part 2: AI & Science

In Part 2, students use AI the way scientists increasingly do: as an assistant
for designing experiments, analysing data, programming and reading scientific
literature. The lessons cover physics, mathematics, chemistry, biology and
robotics. In every lesson, students stay in charge of the science: they check
what the AI suggests, and they draw their own conclusions from their own data.

## The testing week

We tested Part 2 in September 2026 during a week at the 2nd Lyceum of Kos in
Greece, with students from Denmark, Greece and Spain. Most lessons are built around eight real exoplanets. Each group of
students was assigned one planet and studied it throughout the week from the
perspective of different subjects: in physics they determined its size from its
transit, in mathematics they worked out its orbit, in chemistry they analysed a
sample collected by a rover with the help of an AI lab, and in biology they
designed an organism that could survive its conditions. The week started with
robotics, in which students taught a rover to learn by itself. The background
information on the eight planets is in the [Exoplanet Files](exoplanet-files/index.md).

## Using the lessons in your own teaching

Each lesson can also be used on its own. For the lessons that use the
exoplanets, simply give each group one of the eight planet files. The lessons
are longer than in Part 1: most take one or two double lessons, and mathematics
can be split over two sessions.
