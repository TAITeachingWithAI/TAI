# Part 1: AI & Arts and Humanities

In Part 1, students explore how generative AI works and what it means for
creative and cultural work. They first look under the hood of the two main types
of generative AI: large language models, which generate text, and diffusion
models, which generate images and sound. They then use these tools themselves in
languages, literature, history, art and music, always with a critical eye: what
does the AI do well, where does it fail, and who is responsible for the result?

## The testing week

We tested Part 1 in April 2026 during a week at NEXT Vibenshus Gymnasium in
Copenhagen, with students from Denmark, Greece and Spain. The week followed a
clear line. On the first day, students learned how large language models and
diffusion models work, and where language models fail. In the following days,
they applied this knowledge in the subject lessons: prompting language models,
art styles and image prompting, languages, literature, history and sound
generation. The week ended with two larger projects that brought everything
together: a debate about AI, and an exhibition of AI-based artworks on the theme
"The Future: perspectives of the youth".

## Using the lessons in your own teaching

All lessons can also be used on their own, in a regular school timetable. Most
of them take one or two lessons of 45 minutes. We recommend starting with
*How LLMs work*, and with *Diffusion models* before the lessons
about images and sound, because the other lessons build on that understanding.
