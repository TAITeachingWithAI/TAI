# AI in the lessons

In our lessons, students learn *about* AI and learn *with* AI. They use AI in many
different roles: as a tutor, as a research assistant, as a programming partner,
as a creative tool and even as a simulated laboratory. On this page you find
these roles, with the lessons in which students use AI in that way. In all
lessons, students stay in charge: they check what the AI produces and take
responsibility for the result.

## AI as a personal tutor

An AI chatbot adapts to the student: it asks questions, gives feedback and
explains at the student's own level.

- [Physics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/physics/index.md): Emmy, an AI research mentor, guides students through their own investigation with questions instead of a recipe.
- [Languages](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/languages/index.md): a language model assesses the student's level and gives a personalised language lesson.
- [Literature](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/literature/index.md): the AI finds texts that match the student's own interests and helps analyse them.

## AI as a role-play partner

The AI plays a character, so that students can question and challenge it.

- [History](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/history/index.md): students debate a historical decision with an AI that plays the historical figure.

## AI as a research assistant

Students use AI to find, explain and summarise information, and learn to check
it against reliable sources.

- [Physics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/physics/index.md): initial research on exoplanets with an AI assistant, checking its answers against reliable sources.
- [Art styles](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/art-styles/index.md): identifying the style of a mystery artwork with AI, and verifying the answers with museum sources.
- [Biology](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/biology/index.md): using AI to read and understand a real scientific paper.

## AI for data analysis and programming

AI helps students write code, analyse data and make graphs, while students check
whether the results make sense.

- [Physics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/physics/index.md): analysing light-sensor data in a notebook, with AI help to adapt the code.
- [Mathematics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/mathematics/index.md): fitting models and training a neural network in Python, with notebooks that students complete with an AI assistant.
- [Robotics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/robotics/index.md): programming a Cutebot robot with the help of an AI, at three levels of guidance.

## AI as a simulated laboratory

An AI simulates experiments that would be impossible, too slow or too dangerous
in the classroom.

- [Chemistry](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/chemistry/index.md): in the Rover Lab, students run quick virtual experiments on a sample from their exoplanet, before working in the real lab.

## AI as a creative tool

Students create images, sound and texts with generative AI, and learn how to
describe what they want in a prompt.

- [Art styles](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/art-styles/index.md): creating images in a specific art style, and guessing the prompt behind someone else's image.
- [Sound generation](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/sound-generation/index.md): creating sound effects for a silent film clip.
- [Art exhibition](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/art-exhibition/index.md): creating artworks for an exhibition, with the students in charge of the creative process.

## Understanding how AI works

Students look under the hood of AI, often by playing the role of the AI
themselves.

- [How LLMs work](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/how-llms-work/index.md): how a large language model generates text, word by word.
- [Diffusion models](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/diffusion-models/index.md): how AI generates images and sound out of noise.
- [Mathematics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/mathematics/index.md): what machine learning and neural networks are, and how they are trained.
- [Robotics](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/robotics/index.md): how a robot learns by itself through reinforcement learning.

## Using AI critically and responsibly

Students learn when to trust AI, how to get better answers, and what AI means for
society.

- [Where LLMs fail](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/where-llms-fail/index.md): finding out which tasks are hard for a language model.
- [Prompting LLMs](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/prompting-llms/index.md): writing better prompts, and staying responsible for the answer.
- [Biology](for-teachers/part-2-ai-science/biology/index.md): critically evaluating an organism designed by AI.
- [Debate](for-teachers/part-1-ai-arts-and-humanities/debate/index.md): debating ethical questions about AI, such as copyright for AI-made works.
